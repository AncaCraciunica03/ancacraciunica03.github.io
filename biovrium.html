The Question

Does AI in VR actually make a difference?

Microbiology asks students to understand things they can never see with the naked eye. Bacteria. Viruses. Cell structures. Traditional education handles this with diagrams and descriptions. It works, but it does not exactly inspire wonder.

VR changes what is possible. But does adding AI to VR actually make a meaningful difference to learning outcomes? That was the real question. So I built BioVRium twice. Once with AI. Once without. Then I ran two studies to find out which one worked better.

The answer, statistically speaking, was clear.

The Platform

Four levels. Two versions. One very determined student.

BioVRium is a four-level immersive learning environment built in Unreal Engine 5.3.2. Virtual Classroom. Inside the Microscope. Virus World. Bacteria World. Students explore 3D models, interact with content, complete built-in quizzes, and talk to AI characters powered by CONVAI.

All testing sessions were self-organised and self-funded. No university lab. No budget. Just a lot of determination and a very patient group of participants.

The AI Characters

Three characters. Three ways of learning.

Rather than a single AI guide, I designed three distinct characters informed by Study 1 findings on how different people prefer to learn.

BioBear
The Gamified Guide
An animated teddy bear designed to make learning feel like play. Reduces intimidation around complex scientific content through gamification and a friendly, low-stakes environment.
Anca
The Knowledgeable Friend
A young microbiologist who is both expert and approachable. Bridges rigorous academic knowledge with a friendly, relatable presence. Ended up being the favourite.
Dr. Ellis
The Academic Authority
A senior professor for learners who prefer structure and authority. Deep expertise, reliable, thorough. For the students who want to feel like they are in a proper lecture.

Study 1

31 participants. One clear direction.

An online survey distributed through a professional VR research community to understand user needs, preferences, and pain points around microbiology learning and VR usage.

  • 54.8% of participants wanted realistic 3D models, interactive content, and adaptive learning paths. All three, simultaneously. All of which went into the platform.
  • The majority rated built-in quizzes as very important, so both versions of BioVRium included them with immediate feedback after each answer.
  • Users preferred session durations around 30 minutes. BioVRium sessions were designed at 20 to 25 minutes, balancing user preference with research suggesting under 15 minutes is optimal.
Study 2

Crossover study. 10 participants. Statistically significant.

Each participant experienced both versions of BioVRium in randomised order. Pre and post knowledge assessments, SUS usability scores, observational notes, and audio recordings were all collected.

p=.026
Statistical significance, Mann-Whitney U=24
r=0.47
Medium-to-large effect size, meaningful real-world impact

The AI-enhanced version produced significantly higher learning outcomes and usability scores. Almost every participant preferred it. Most preferred the character Anca, despite her being the only one with a non-human voice. Which tells you something interesting about what people actually respond to in AI design.

Honest About What Did Not Work

Good research includes the uncomfortable bits.

"Without AI because I did not need to wait for a long period to know what the answer to the question asked is."
Participant 6

The CONVAI plugin had real-time responsiveness limitations. Some participants found AI response delays frustrating. One preferred the version without AI entirely because of this. These are legitimate technical constraints, not design failures. The conceptual finding stands. The technical constraint is solvable.

What I Took Away

AI in educational VR shows genuine, measurable promise.

  • AI integration meaningfully improves engagement and learning outcomes. Not just subjectively. Statistically.
  • Diversity in character design matters. Different people responded to different personas, validating the three-character approach from the start.
  • Users value visual humanisation over vocal realism. Anca looked human but did not sound it, and she was still the favourite. That is a counterintuitive finding with real implications for AI character design.
  • Self-funded, independently run research is possible and produces publishable-quality findings. You just have to want the answer badly enough.
