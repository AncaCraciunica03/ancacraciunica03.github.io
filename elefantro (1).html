Elefant.ro —

I want to be upfront about something. This was not a client project. No one hired me to do this. I was using Elefant.ro, Romania's biggest online bookstore, and the UX was frustrating me enough that I spent a Saturday redesigning it. That is either a character flaw or a professional one. Probably both.

The Context

I could not switch off the analytical brain.

Elefant.ro is Romania's leading online retailer. Books, e-books, stationery, lifestyle products. I used it regularly. And regularly I found myself frustrated by its navigation complexity, cluttered homepage, and complete lack of discovery features.

The instinct to spot a conversion problem in something you use every day and actually do something about it turns out to be exactly the same instinct that produces good marketing work. The context changes. The thinking does not.

User persona developed from understanding the typical Elefant.ro browser
The Problem

Users knew what they wanted. They could not find it.

Research found that many users arrived with a general category in mind. They wanted Romanian fiction, or science books, or a gift for someone. But the site's complexity made navigation feel like work. The opportunity was to reduce that friction and introduce discovery mechanics that rewarded browsing rather than punishing it.

The Solution

Four changes. Each targeting a specific conversion lever.

Featured carousel
Central hero carousel for new releases and featured titles. Immediate attention capture on landing. Gives the homepage a focal point it did not have before.
Simplified category navigation
Clear category section with quick links to Sale and Bestseller. Fewer decisions between landing and buying intent.
Book Roulette
Randomised book suggestion feature for people who want to discover something new without knowing exactly what they are looking for. Turns passive browsing into active exploration.
Animated discount mechanic
An animated gift box that reveals a discount code on click. A small moment of delight at exactly the right point in the journey is worth more than a banner nobody reads.

What I Would Test

Hypotheses worth validating.

I would measure task completion rate, time on task, and interaction with Book Roulette specifically. My hypothesis is that the discovery mechanic increases average session time and reduces the gap between browsing and purchasing for users who arrive without a specific title in mind.

Mobile view of the redesigned experience
What I Took Away

Visual hierarchy and conversion mechanics are inseparable.

  • Discovery features like Book Roulette address a real user behaviour that most e-commerce sites ignore entirely. Not everyone knows what they want when they arrive. Design for that person too.
  • Gamified discount mechanics create micro-moments of delight that directly influence purchase intent. An animated reveal beats a static banner every time.
  • Simplifying navigation is not about removing categories. It is about surfacing the right ones at the right moment in the user journey.
  • A one-day project done from genuine user frustration often produces sharper thinking than a month-long brief. The problem was real and personal. That made the solution more honest.
