HouseHive —
A New Standard
in Floorplan Security

Here is something nobody seems to talk about. When you browse a property listing online, the floorplan is right there. Free. No verification. No friction. Anyone can see the exact layout of someone's home. That bothered me. So I did something about it.

The Problem

The real estate industry has a quiet security problem.

Detailed floorplans, freely accessible to anyone with an internet connection, are a gift to people who should not have them. Meanwhile, the same platforms handing over this information use dark patterns and impenetrable privacy policies to obscure what they do with yours.

HouseHive was my attempt to imagine what an ethical, secure property platform could actually look like.

Early wireframes showing the home page, floorplan restriction screen, and search results
The Research

Users wanted security. They just did not expect it to feel easy.

Surveys and competitive analysis of existing property platforms revealed a consistent pattern. Users were aware of the security risk but assumed that adding verification would create a frustrating experience. The design challenge was to prove them wrong.

Users expressed concern over the ease of access to detailed floorplans without sufficient verification, but equally feared that security measures would make the platform unusable. The solution had to be both secure and seamless. Not one or the other.

The Solution

Verification that does not feel like a barrier.

HouseHive introduced OTP authentication for floorplan access. Simple, familiar, fast. You verify once, you see the floorplan. No verification, no floorplan. The security is real but the friction is minimal.

Beyond that, every piece of communication in HouseHive was rewritten from scratch. Privacy policies in plain English. Key clauses highlighted. No misleading copy. No dark patterns. Just honest information presented clearly.

What Testing Showed

Security and satisfaction, together.

Users felt more secure. They also found the platform more trustworthy, which turns out to be a competitive advantage in an industry that has historically treated user trust as an afterthought. Navigation was smoother. Satisfaction was higher. And nobody complained that the verification step was too much effort.

What I Took Away

Security and usability are not opposites.

  • They only feel that way when security is treated as a barrier rather than a feature. Frame it as protection and users welcome it.
  • Ethical communication is a design decision, not a legal formality. Most platforms treat privacy policies as compliance documents. They should be product features.
  • Users trust platforms that respect their intelligence. Plain language outperforms legal jargon every time.
  • Most property platforms are leaving an enormous amount of user trust on the table. The first one to change that has a genuine competitive advantage.
