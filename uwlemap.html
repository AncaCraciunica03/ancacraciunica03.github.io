The Problem

Generic maps fail specific people.

University of West London's campus is large and complex. New students and visitors consistently struggled to find their way and physical maps were not helping. The existing navigation solutions were static, impersonal, and completely indifferent to where a student actually needed to be.

The Research

The problem was not maps. It was personalisation.

A first year nursing student and a third year graphic design student have completely different daily routes. A generic map serves neither of them particularly well. What they actually needed was a map that started from them, not from the building.

How might we create a more intuitive campus navigation experience that adapts to who the user is, not just where they want to go?

User persona developed from research findings
The Solution

A map that knows your timetable.

When you open UWL E-Map for the first time, it asks you two questions. What are you studying? What year are you in? From those answers, it builds a personalised campus map with your relevant classrooms already highlighted. Select a classroom. Get directions. Done. No searching through directories, no trying to decipher a static PDF.

What I Took Away

Personalisation is not a feature. It is the foundation.

  • Designing for everyone usually means designing for no one well enough. The moment you ask who someone actually is and build from that answer, everything gets clearer.
  • Usability testing confirmed the personalised approach directly improved navigation confidence. People stopped feeling lost.
  • Accessibility was a constraint from the start, not an afterthought. That shaped every interaction model in the app.
  • Gathering requirements properly upfront reduced the number of design iterations needed. Research is not a delay. It is the shortcut.
